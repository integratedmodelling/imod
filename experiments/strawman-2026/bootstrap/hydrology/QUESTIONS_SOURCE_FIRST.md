# Hydrology source-led questions — pass A

Written before this iteration's hydrology candidate records. The same author has seen the earlier catchment packet: this is not an independent holdout set. Order is retained in Q01–Q15. Source pages accessed 2026-10-03; USGS Water Science School is introductory primary agency material, not specialist consensus or permission to adopt every simplification.

1. Which land drains towards the bridge's river outlet, and is that the same area supplying groundwater to the well? — USGS Watersheds, common-outlet definition and groundwater paragraph; deliberately distinguishes the source's broader watershed wording.
2. How much water is stored in this lake now? — USGS Streamflow, storage versus moving water.
3. Is water crossing the river section faster after the storm, or is there simply more of it? — USGS Streamflow, discharge and stage distinctions.
4. How much rain soaks into the ground instead of running over it? — USGS Infiltration, infiltration versus recharge/runoff.
5. Does this stream receive groundwater or lose water into the ground at this reach? — USGS Streamflow, groundwater discharge/recharge mechanisms.
6. Can groundwater stored beneath one surface catchment feed a stream in another? — USGS Groundwater and Watersheds; scope probe requiring independent subsurface boundaries, not presumed equality.
7. Why is the river still flowing after several rainless weeks? — USGS Runoff/Infiltration, baseflow; explanation requires a model, not a name.
8. Did the storm produce an actual flood beyond the channel, or only a high river reading? — USGS Floods and Streamflow; local delimitation required.
9. Did the lake lose water through evaporation during the hot spell? — USGS Streamflow, mechanisms of change; separate change observation and attribution.
10. Are the two tributaries physically joined even while one is dry? — USGS Watersheds, drainage network; structural connectivity versus observed transfer.
11. Has water volume fallen during pumping, and can the observations distinguish pumping from other losses? — USGS Groundwater and Streamflow withdrawals; causal attribution not automatic.
12. After wildfire, does the next storm carry more sediment into the stream? — USGS Runoff, erosion; cross-domain prediction is a model gap, not guaranteed fire effect.
13. Is this river unusually low for this season, or has an agreed drought episode actually begun? — WMO/UNESCO hydrology glossary metadata discovered; term-specific reading still outstanding, so source and threshold gap retained.
14. Can a dry reach become connected again during a bounded wetting episode? — USGS Streamflow variability; proposed event boundary is author's modeling choice.
15. If no change estimate is available at the next time transition, what can we still say about lake storage? — User's explicit open-world change constraint; deliberately tests machinery boundary rather than hydrological fact.

Sources: [Watersheds](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/watersheds-and-drainage-basins), [Streamflow](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/streamflow-and-water-cycle), [Infiltration](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/infiltration-and-water-cycle), [Runoff](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/runoff-surface-and-overland-water-runoff), [Groundwater](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/groundwater-what-groundwater), [Floods](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/floods-and-recurrence-intervals), [WMO glossary metadata](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark%3A/48223/pf0000221862).

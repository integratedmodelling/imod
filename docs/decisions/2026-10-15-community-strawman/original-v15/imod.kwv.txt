/**
 * # Integrated Modelling worldview
 *
 * The root introduces the shared vocabulary of observation, the configurations
 * through which systems are recognized, and the domains that organize the
 * worldview. Its declarations are universals. Models and digital twins supply
 * particular entities, observations and bindings between instances.
 *
 * Read the opening sections in order: system configurations establish a common
 * organizational vocabulary; physical anchors identify subject matter; named
 * Knowledge domains introduce descriptive perspectives on that subject matter.
 * The observation kinds and general predicates that follow are shared by the
 * disciplinary namespaces. The strawman supplies a coherent starting vocabulary,
 * while leaving detailed scientific articulation to subsequent community work.
 *
 * ## Three distinct semantic relations
 *
 * `about` identifies a Knowledge domain's physical subject matter. `describes`
 * relates a description to its subject, including a Domain describing a
 * Configuration. `of` specifies bearer inherency. Selecting a domain does not
 * make every owned quality inhere in that domain's subject matter, and it does
 * not turn subjects, events or qualities into Configurations.
 *
 * ## Reading and writing this worldview
 *
 * A quoted declaration docstring gives the concept's meaning. Literate Markdown
 * comments explain the scope, sequence and rationale of related declarations;
 * they should read as a connected account rather than repeat every docstring.
 * Use qualified names in backticks when referring to concepts, and ordinary
 * Markdown links for sources that support a specific claim or design decision.
 * Explain conceptual distinctions where they help the reader interpret a
 * declaration, and keep each namespace's account within its stated scope.
 *
 * The numbered ODO 0.1.1 foundation supplies the imported kinds. The root's
 * aliases expose those kinds in the worldview vocabulary; the named domains and
 * system configurations organize their use in the namespaces that follow.
 */
ontology imod
   "Shared observation concepts, system configurations and domain interfaces for the Integrated Modelling worldview."
   version 1.0

/**
 * # Systems and Earth-system organization
 *
 * The core alias precedes the ordinary configurations that use it. System is
 * generic; EarthSystem supplies the Earth-specific intermediate. Sphere
 * configurations may overlap and are neither exhaustive nor disjoint.
 *
 * PhysicalSystem is retained as a universal specialization of System, without
 * an intrinsic Earth restriction. EarthSystem specializes PhysicalSystem; its six
 * sphere configurations retain their existing PhysicalSystem ancestry.
 * PhysicalSystem is a Configuration, distinct from a Physical domain. It need not
 * be the parent of every physical phenomenon.
 *
 * System qualification or inherency belongs on physical-domain expressions.
 * Knowledge domains identify physical subject matter with about, rather than
 * inhering in a system Configuration. Configuration description with describes
 * is a separate relation; it does not supply ABOUT grounding or bind instances.
 */
abstract configuration Configuration
    "Configurations represent emergent patterns (aggregates of other entities) that are generated by observations of 
     quality and relationship."
   is core odo:Configuration;

abstract configuration System
    "An observationally recognized configuration expressing the organization of
     interacting parts and their relationships through identifiable patterns."
    is imod:Configuration;

configuration PhysicalSystem
    "A system configuration expressing organization of physical phenomena at a
     stated observational scope, without an intrinsic restriction to Earth."
    is imod:System;

configuration EarthSystem
    "A system configuration expressing organization of Earth phenomena at a
     stated observational scope, including their interactions."
    is imod:PhysicalSystem
    has children
        Atmosphere
            "Earth-system organization of atmospheric gases, layers and exchanges.",
        Hydrosphere
            "Earth-system organization of water in its physical settings and states.",
        Geosphere
            "Earth-system organization of solid materials, structures and interactions.",
        Biosphere
            "Earth-system organization of living organisms and their environmental interactions.",
        Anthroposphere
            "Earth-system organization of human populations, activities and social relationships.",
        Technosphere
            "Earth-system organization of human-made artifacts and built networks.";

/**
 * # Domain kinds and physical subject matter
 *
 * The four core aliases expose imported kinds; they have no additional clauses.
 * Physical, Knowledge and Normative are pairwise disjoint and non-exhaustive.
 * Only ordinary physical-domain specializations carry system qualification here.
 * Knowledge domains use `about` to identify physical subject matter, independently
 * of configuration description and the bearers of their owned qualities.
 *
 * An ontology may select the exact generic Knowledge category when its vocabulary
 * spans physical subjects, as in data's observation-scaling predicates. A named
 * specialization must instead identify explicit or inherited physical subject
 * matter, even when abstract. Concrete Knowledge-owned qualities still need an
 * effective description; generic category ownership does not waive that contract.
 */
abstract domain Domain
    "The field or perspective within which owned concepts are articulated."
    is core odo:Domain;

abstract domain Physical
    "A domain articulating physical phenomena."
    is core odo:PhysicalDomain;

abstract domain Knowledge
    "A descriptive or explanatory domain whose specializations identify physical subject matter."
    is core odo:KnowledgeDomain;

abstract domain Normative
    "A domain articulating prescriptions, requirements or permissions within an identifiable framework."
    is core odo:NormativeDomain;

/**
 * # Shared physical anchors
 *
 * These anchors provide subject matter, not full disciplinary taxonomies.
 * PhysicalPhenomena, Life, Society and BuiltEnvironment are universal in scope;
 * Earth alone introduces Earth-system scope. Their system qualification does not
 * bind every owned quality to one bearer or assert a shared particular witness.
 * About and describes remain independent relations.
 */
domain PhysicalPhenomena
    "Physical phenomena considered through physical-system organization, without an intrinsic restriction to Earth."
    is imod:Physical of imod:PhysicalSystem
    describes imod:PhysicalSystem;

domain Earth
    "Physical Earth phenomena considered within Earth-system organization."
    is imod:PhysicalPhenomena of imod:EarthSystem
    describes imod:EarthSystem;

domain Life
    "Living phenomena and their physical settings, without an intrinsic restriction to Earth."
    is imod:PhysicalPhenomena;

domain Society
    "Observed social phenomena and collective organization, without an intrinsic restriction to Earth."
    is imod:PhysicalPhenomena;

domain BuiltEnvironment
    "Built artifacts and networks in their settings, uses and functioning, without an intrinsic restriction to Earth."
    is imod:PhysicalPhenomena;

/**
 * # Named descriptive domains
 *
 * Each ordinary specialization names a descriptive perspective and explicitly
 * identifies physical subject matter. Repeated `about` modifiers retain separate
 * links: Ecology concerns both Life and PhysicalPhenomena, while Land connects
 * Earth, Society and Life. `about (A and B)` would instead name one composite
 * target. These declarations add necessary constraints, not reverse definitions,
 * a shared instance witness, or a computation.
 *
 * Geography describes Earth locations, terrain and regional distinctions.
 * Valuation describes societal preferences across many contexts, including
 * economic, ethical, aesthetic and health concerns. Describing a preference is
 * different from prescribing what ought to be valued. A Normative domain
 * identifies the framework within which prescriptions are articulated.
 *
 * `AtmosphereKnowledge` distinguishes the descriptive domain from the existing
 * `Atmosphere` Configuration. Both names are deliberate: one identifies a
 * perspective on Earth phenomena, the other their recognized system organization.
 */
domain Agency
    "Observed agency and deliberate activity in their social setting."
    is imod:Knowledge about imod:Society;

domain AtmosphereKnowledge
    "Descriptive and explanatory knowledge of Earth atmospheric phenomena."
    is imod:Knowledge about imod:Earth;

domain Biology
    "Descriptive and explanatory knowledge of living structure, function and occurrences."
    is imod:Knowledge about imod:Life;

domain Chemistry
    "Descriptive and explanatory knowledge of composition and chemical transformations, without an intrinsic restriction to Earth."
    is imod:Knowledge about imod:PhysicalPhenomena;

domain Ecology
    "Descriptive and explanatory knowledge of living interactions and their physical settings."
    is imod:Knowledge about imod:Life about imod:PhysicalPhenomena;

domain Economics
    "Descriptive and explanatory knowledge of observed production, exchange and accounting."
    is imod:Knowledge about imod:Society;

domain Engineering
    "Descriptive and explanatory knowledge of built artifacts and their observed operation."
    is imod:Knowledge about imod:BuiltEnvironment;

domain Genetics
    "Descriptive and explanatory knowledge of genomic bearers, inheritance and molecular occurrences."
    is imod:Knowledge about imod:Life;

domain Geography
    "Descriptive and explanatory knowledge of Earth locations, terrain and spatial organization, including geographical and regional identities."
    is imod:Knowledge about imod:Earth;

domain Geology
    "Descriptive and explanatory knowledge of Earth geological bodies, materials and occurrences."
    is imod:Knowledge about imod:Earth;

domain Hydrology
    "Descriptive and explanatory knowledge of Earth water bodies, flow and storage."
    is imod:Knowledge about imod:Earth;

domain Land
    "Descriptive knowledge of Earth land cover, use and management; physical, social and living subject matter remain distinct."
    is imod:Knowledge about imod:Earth about imod:Society about imod:Life;

domain Oceanography
    "Descriptive and explanatory knowledge of Earth marine bodies and their conditions."
    is imod:Knowledge about imod:Earth;

domain Physics
    "Descriptive and explanatory knowledge of physical quantities and processes, without an intrinsic restriction to Earth."
    is imod:Knowledge about imod:PhysicalPhenomena;

domain Sociology
    "Descriptive and explanatory knowledge of observed social relations and processes."
    is imod:Knowledge about imod:Society;

domain Soil
    "Descriptive and explanatory knowledge of Earth soils, horizons and their physical settings."
    is imod:Knowledge about imod:Earth;

domain Valuation
    "Descriptive knowledge of societal preferences and value attribution across economic, ethical, aesthetic, health and other contexts, including valued assets and their associated hazards and risks."
    is imod:Knowledge about imod:Society;

## Evidence and discriminating scenario

[USGS Groundwater Basics](https://www.usgs.gov/mission-areas/water-resources/science/groundwater-basics) defines groundwater by saturated pores/cracks and permits settings beneath oceans; [USGS Water Resources Glossaries](https://water.usgs.gov/water-basics_glossary.html) records broader historical and usual saturated-zone groundwater usage, distinguishes unsaturated soil moisture and surface water. Both retrieved2026-10-23; source-backed narrower Groundwater plus broader SubsurfaceWater prevents calling all vadose water groundwater. These sources do not prescribe ontology placement. [TEOS-10](https://www.teos-10.org/) seawater composition/salinity distinctions do not justify pure-H2O identity equivalence. See prior naming record for retrieval limits on additional sources.

A groundwater twin observes an identified Groundwater body, a river twin observes SurfaceWater, and a coastal twin observes Seawater. Common Water ancestry supports semantic subsumption across these independently established bodies. Observed groundwater->river and river->marine transfers may use TransfersWaterTo endpoints. Material, geometry, support, time and units must remain compatible; this does not implement a digital-twin transport API or establish a transfer from adjacency. Aquifer host rock, geographic WaterBody and a moving parcel are distinct. Soil/vadose water is not inferred Groundwater merely from subsurface position; saturated-zone evidence matters. Seawater is not inferred SurfaceWater, and saline groundwater is not automatically Seawater. No inferred disjointness is claimed.
